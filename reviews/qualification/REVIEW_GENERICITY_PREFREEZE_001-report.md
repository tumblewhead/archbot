## Repair ordering challenge

The proposed sequence is mostly defensible because it reviews the audit before modifying shared tools, and keeps a final integrated freeze review after blocker evidence. The ordering needs a narrow adjustment: define the cleanroom threat model, positive allowlist, and visibility tests early, but defer building/testing the physical cleanroom pack until project context selection, domain-gate policy, and launch boundaries are parameterized. Otherwise the pack may encode the current AS-001 defaults and require rebuilding. The proposed P0-B can remain an isolation specification/bootstrap design task; its package digest and sentinel execution should follow the generic separation work.

Likewise, P0-C should qualify the execution mode or modes actually promised by the process. A direct review in this invocation shows this review can be carried out directly; it is not a cleanroom launch trial. The historical outer-shell failures remain evidence of failed attempts, not proof that every future outer-shell environment fails. Do not require two successful modes merely for symmetry, and do not call either mode generally qualified based on this review.

An independent review can meaningfully accept the audit's traceability and bounded analysis without qualifying an execution path. The allowed conclusion is only acceptance of the candidate audit within its declared scope; it cannot resolve the framework blockers or authorize repairs by itself.
