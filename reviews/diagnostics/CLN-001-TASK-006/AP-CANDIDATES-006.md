# CLN-001 architectural candidates 006

Status: **AP_CANDIDATE_NOT_SELECTED** for every candidate below. Proposal authoring only; no eligibility determination, proof completion, contract approval, DE/CON, C implementation, or architecture selection. The accepted synthetic baseline is AS-REV-002 through AS-ACTIVATION-002, D001, accepted F through F-DISPOSITION-003, mathematical claims through PROOF-DISPOSITION-004 (PD), and D002. Their later dispositions govern the older draft/pending language retained in source documents. Historical records named by those inputs were not consulted.

## Shared observable and limits

For accepted samples in one initialized/reset sequence, `x_i` is an unsigned integer in `[0,4095]`. Specification-only positions `j <= 0` have `x_j=0`. Each accepted event `n>=1` requires exactly `(n,E_n)`, where `E_n = sum(x_(n-k)^2, k=0..31)`. Initial mathematical energy is zero; publication before the first event is optional. Explicit reset starts the relation afresh. A logical index is neither a timestamp nor an approved stored counter.

Out-of-domain input must expose rejection/precondition violation without advancing numeric history, accepted index, or other detector sequence state. Diagnostic reporting must not mutate that sequence state. Range validation must precede narrowing or squaring. Invalid input is never a zero-valued accepted event. Intended delivery is ordered and omission-free under D001; duplicates, gaps and disorder are outside that contract. Detectable faults must be reported; an interface without source identifiers has no promised duplicate/gap detection. No fault-recovery, automatic reset, operational-loss padding, or silent sample deletion is proposed as approved behavior.

The publisher owns acquisition timing. Nominal 1 ms arrivals impose no hard elapsed-time bound. Nonetheless, indefinite service stalls, finite queues and result loss cannot be justified merely by the absence of a deadline. Each proposal needs later acceptance/publication coordination so every accepted event has one result and resets do not interleave incoherently. Receiving a valid sample is not permission to discard it by redefining acceptance. Backpressure, buffering, crash recovery and availability are unresolved contract questions, not established features.

All proposals use exact integer operations conceptually and need no hardware FPU by construction intent. This is not build evidence. For the adopted domain:

- `Qmax = 4095^2 = 16,769,025 < 2^24`.
- `31 Qmax = 519,839,775 < 2^29` and `32 Qmax = 536,608,800 < 2^29`.
- A square therefore needs at least 24 unsigned value bits and a total at least 29; abstract unsigned 32-bit arithmetic can represent these values. Source-language promotions, multiplication before widening, signedness, accumulator order, packing, stale state and index arithmetic remain realization risks. No compiler or instruction sequence is selected.

## Logical-index refinement choices, still blocking

The exact abstract pair is preserved as an obligation, not replaced with a bounded index. Two prospective physical refinements are explored:

**RI: ordered event association.** A physical transaction delivers the energy on the response associated with its accepted request, with an observable initialization/reset boundary and no omitted, duplicated or reordered accepted responses. The proposed abstraction map assigns `n = rank of that accepted request since the boundary` and maps the associated response to `(n,E_n)`. Rank is a mathematical map over an arbitrarily long trace, not a hidden detector counter. The surrounding consumer must retain or reconstruct the association across its own lifetime; its queues, reset synchronization and any stored counts are inventoried separately. Whether this implicit association actually satisfies D001's requirement to supply an index needs D003/additive AS clarification. It is not assumed accepted merely because the map is writable. If a numeral must cross the interface, RI alone is insufficient.

**RN: external exact numeral association.** A prospective publisher/index component associates each accepted event with its exact positive integer ordinal, resetting to one for the next accepted event after explicit reset. A physical response associates `E_n` with a lossless, potentially variable-length representation of that ordinal. The refinement decodes the representation to `n`; protocol framing must distinguish events and reset sequences. No fixed field length, finite sequence duration, wrap, mandatory reset, or existing service is assumed. Streaming might bound local staging, but does not establish finite external capacity, availability, parser correctness or bounded total work. Source acquisition ordinals cannot substitute for accepted ordinals when invalid inputs occur. Owner, numeral generation, validation, transport, failure behavior and required authority remain unresolved.

For all candidates an ordinary finite local counter, without another refinement, would leave exact unbounded indexing unresolved. PO-003-3 remains UNPROVEN; this document establishes no new impossibility theorem. D002-5 requires reviewed dependencies and separate authority for operating restrictions. If neither RI nor a reviewed RN-like service is accepted and feasible, no full-interface feasibility claim follows from numeric history sufficiency.

## AP-006-A — Local raw-tail recomputation

**AP_CANDIDATE_NOT_SELECTED**. Structural idea: the detector retains the ordered latest-31 raw samples and recomputes each full energy from them and the incoming sample. It trades repeated arithmetic for a direct representation of the accepted history.

**Boundary and information.** At a quiescent event boundary, A-NUM contains 31 ordered raw values (including initial padding), their order/cursor metadata, and all numeric control state. On a valid event, conceptually sum the square of the new value and each of the 31 retained values, then discard the oldest tail value and append the new one. This is a candidate transition rationale, not a proof. The previous emitted total need not be retained for future numeric outputs. Current input, reduction accumulator, any staged result and transaction status exist while processing and count toward memory. Downstream copies of old outputs are not read back as history.

History is detector-owned, retained until it leaves the 31-entry tail or explicit reset occurs. Startup/reset establishes 31 logical zeros and a consistent order. Physical clearing versus validity metadata is a later choice; its RAM and reset work must be counted. Raw entries, cursor, staging and output metadata must be validated/maintained consistently. A corrupt cursor or stale reset view is not a valid implementation of the proposal.

**Acceptance and delivery.** Validate before tail mutation; rejection leaves tail, order and association unchanged. Process accepted events in publisher order and expose every result. RI is the initial physical-interface exploration: event-linked response plus observable reset, with the exact rank map above. Numeric processing must not commit a new history while silently losing that event's response. Whether staging, a caller rendezvous or another coordination mechanism can meet this is unresolved. The publisher and consumer are the external dependencies even though history is local. If they cannot identify source gaps, A cannot infer them from values. If they can identify them, report faults; do not silently insert zeros or restart.

**Index and faults.** RI has no approved implementation or approved sufficiency interpretation. An RN adapter would be an additional dependency and additional owned/shared costs, not a free replacement. Disconnect, reset races, response loss and buffer exhaustion need visible fault reporting and an authorized recovery contract. A reported fault does not discharge an already accepted event's required result.

**Memory and arithmetic.** Raw storage has symbolic size `31 b_x` bytes, plus ordering/control, persistent transaction/index association state, live scratch, queues and owned callee/stack costs. `b_x` is a declared representation size, not a measured allocation. Any local counter or trace retention must be included; unbounded local numeral growth cannot be hidden in a constant overhead. See the separate F worksheet. A computes 32 squares and a reduction; all partial sums can stay within the full-window bound if operations use the required exact widths. Narrow squaring and accumulator initialization remain explicit risks. Actual RAM fit and no-FPU target behavior are UNKNOWN.

**PD applicability.** The raw padded tail directly represents T1's information for future nonempty numeric continuations; T1 does not cover the already emitted total, index, reset framing or transaction metadata. T2 applies conditionally to complete A-NUM at fixed `n>=32` if every numeric output-affecting item is counted, determinism and unrestricted continuations hold, and no output/history replay is read externally. RI's external ordinal association alone does not remove the fixed-n numeric lower bound. Closure of the full physical interface is not claimed. The 372-bit/47-octet capacity minimum is neither a sufficient allocation nor a total RAM estimate.

**Unestablished contracts/authority.** RI sufficiency, reset/commit association, delivery coordination and any identifier or RN addition require later review; changed accepted behavior needs new sponsor-delegate authority. T002-2..8 and R4 remain open as mapped in AP-COMPARISON-006.

## AP-006-B — Local squared-tail with incremental total

**AP_CANDIDATE_NOT_SELECTED**. Structural idea: retain the ordered latest-31 squared samples plus their aggregate; use an expiration update rather than recomputing the window. This changes retained representation, redundancy, arithmetic and failure sensitivity relative to A. It remains a distinct local numeric design; C below supplies the additional ownership/replay boundary alternative.

**Boundary and information.** B-NUM contains an ordered 31-entry square tail, a total `S` of that tail, ordering metadata and numeric control state. Before event `n`, the proposed invariant is `S = sum(x_j^2, j=n-31..n-1)` with startup padding. Compute `q=x_n^2`, form `E_n=S+q`, and for the next event replace the oldest square `q_old` with `q` and retain `S_next=E_n-q_old`. The emitted energy is the 32-entry total; the retained total is the next 31-entry tail total. This invariant is an obligation for later analysis, not a completed proof.

Because samples are nonnegative, exact squaring is injective on the accepted domain. Ordered squares preserve the raw tail's numeric distinctions; the total alone does not. Aggregate `S` is redundant information that may reduce arithmetic work but must agree with the retained tail. Initialization/reset establishes zeros for both tail and total and a consistent order. State persists until expiration/reset. Current `q`, old square, result, transaction and index metadata count even when transient. Prior emitted results are not an external history source.

**Acceptance, output and faults.** Validate first; invalid input cannot advance the tail, total, cursor or association. Proposed outputs use RI with the same exact rank abstraction and unresolved acceptance interpretation as A. Tail replacement, aggregate update and publication need coherent acceptance/commit semantics. Partial updates, reset races or replayed responses must not be presented as successful accepted transitions. Crash-safe storage or atomicity is not assumed. Publisher/consumer guarantees, fault observability limits, applicable reports and no-loss obligations are the same explicit unresolved dependencies as A, not an implicit service guarantee.

**Index.** No local bounded counter is equated to `n`. RI remains pending D003/additive AS clarification. If RN becomes necessary, its implementation, availability, ownership and costs must be added and reviewed for B as for A; no finite epoch is introduced to make the design fit.

**Memory and arithmetic.** Numeric persistent payload is `31 b_q + b_S`, plus order/control, transaction/index state, scratch and owned stack. A square representation generally consumes more bytes than a raw representation under ordinary unpacked choices, but no concrete representation or fit is asserted. `S+q` is at most `32 Qmax`; subtracting `q_old` is safe only under the candidate invariant. Wrong order, subtracting from the wrong total, an inconsistent aggregate or unsigned underflow can destroy exactness. Correct-width integer arithmetic needs no FPU in the abstract; dependency/build evidence is absent. The worksheet keeps all omitted overhead UNKNOWN rather than zero.

**PD applicability.** The ordered square tail corresponds to T1's latest-31 information; aggregate alone is not T1-sufficient. T2 applies conditionally to complete closed B-NUM under the same fixed-n/no-replay/unrestricted-future hypotheses as A. Redundant total storage does not reduce distinguishability requirements. T1 does not prove the update invariant or index refinement. T2 does not count only the aggregate or assert a 47-byte design.

**Unestablished contracts/authority.** No new service is supplied by having an incremental update. RI, atomic acceptance/publication, reset ordering, response handling and any RN addition require later review/authority as above. T002-2..8 and R4 remain open.

## AP-006-C — Publisher-retained snapshot, streaming recomputation

**AP_CANDIDATE_NOT_SELECTED**. Structural idea: the detector does not retain the numeric tail across events. A prospective publisher history component retains and supplies a consistent pre-event latest-31 snapshot; the detector streams that snapshot and the current value into a fresh exact reduction. This is an external-readable-history architecture, not a smaller claimed closed machine.

**Boundary and information.** C-DET includes validation, a current-event context, reduction state, stream position, snapshot/session references and publication/association control. The prospective upstream component C-HIST owns the chronological accepted tail, startup-validity information, reset context, snapshot/version/retention state, current sample and any retry records. It may also provide RN accepted-ordinal association. Snapshot and index services are not established by D001's ordinary sample-publisher role. Prior energy responses are not a substitute for a complete ordered tail.

For event `n`, the conceptual read supplies exactly `x_(n-31)..x_(n-1)` with zeros only for pre-sequence positions. The current sample supplies the 32nd term. A consistent snapshot must remain readable until the reduction and required response association finish. Subsequent acquisition must not overwrite unread entries. Serial streaming is contemplated to avoid assuming a 31-entry detector buffer; copies, transport buffers, handles and pinned/shared allocations must still be accounted. Bounded active history does not imply bounded snapshot retention under arbitrary concurrency or stalls.

**Lifecycle and validation.** Explicit reset must establish a coherent new publisher history and index sequence and prevent an old snapshot/result from appearing in the new sequence. No fixed-width generation token is presumed immune to reuse. Out-of-domain current input is rejected before acceptance; C-HIST must not append it or advance its accepted ordinal. Received snapshot entries and framing require range, length, order, sequence and version validation to the extent the interface permits. Correct in-range values from the wrong history cannot be detected by range checking alone; service fidelity is an indispensable contract obligation. A malformed replay response is a service fault, not a zero-valued sample or permission to erase accepted events.

**Acceptance, result and failures.** The candidate requires publisher/history and detector coordination so the accepted-history append, snapshot identity, accepted ordinal and response correspond to the same event. Startup has a zero tail; each successful event contributes its new sample to the publisher tail for future reads. Data order and one-result-per-event obligations remain exact. The precise commit/rendezvous and retry mechanism is not authored here. Duplicate messages must not become duplicate accepted events; guaranteeing that may require additional retention and identifiers. Disconnects, unavailable snapshots, premature eviction, mixed versions and detectable delivery faults must be reported. Availability, backpressure, bounded pending work and recovery remain UNKNOWN. After an event has been accepted, merely reporting service loss does not satisfy its missing output. No automatic rejection of otherwise-valid inputs, forced reset or loss filling is authorized.

**Index refinement.** C explores RN: externally generated exact accepted ordinals, physically represented losslessly and associated with each result. Decoding the associated numeral gives precisely `n`; energy comes from the corresponding current sample and snapshot. The service must count accepted events, not ADC attempts or transport messages, and coordinate reset. A streamed numeral avoids asserting a fixed local index field; it does not prove bounded local working memory or bound the external service. Availability, exactness and representation lengths are UNKNOWN. If RI is later authorized instead, that is a separately reviewed variant with recomputed costs, not a silently interchangeable guarantee.

**Ownership and arithmetic.** Only genuinely exclusively upstream-owned allocations may be outside R5. Detector-owned client/transport buffers, stack, library state, index framing, queues and retry state count locally. Shared snapshot allocations cannot automatically be excluded because the publisher allocated them; ownership and attribution need explicit review. Outside history, snapshot retention, transport, ordinal storage and service compute/electrical energy are reported separately. If ownership is unresolved, the detector RAM result stays UNKNOWN. Streaming performs 32 squares and a 31-add reduction under the same integer bounds as A, plus validation and service/index work. No FPU or target-fit evidence is claimed.

**PD applicability.** T1 requires the joint system to preserve or retrieve the exact ordered latest-31 numeric distinctions. A one-value detector accumulator does not abolish that information. T2 is inapplicable as a local C-DET history/RAM lower bound because future outputs consult C-HIST. A larger C-DET+C-HIST boundary could meet T2 only if it includes every numeric output-affecting retained item and has no further readable replay, is deterministic and admits unrestricted continuations at fixed `n>=32`. This closure is unestablished. External indexing by itself is separate from the numeric-history escape from local closure. T2 says nothing about C-HIST availability, RN correctness, or sufficient allocation.

**Unestablished contracts/authority.** A publisher-history/snapshot service and RN service require explicitly reviewed contracts and sponsor-delegate decisions before concrete commitment. Service ownership, reset semantics, consistency, retention, observable faults, acceptance coordination, concurrency and exhaustion behavior are all unresolved. D002 is disclosure authority, not service availability. T002-2..8 and R4 remain open.

Retain A, B and C as alternatives for later independent feasibility analysis. A provides a direct local-history reference; B tests the cost of redundant state against repeated arithmetic; C tests whether a reviewed publisher-history service can justify shifted ownership and replay costs. Keep information and index assumptions separately visible so resolving one does not freeze the other.
