## 4. Countermodels and limits of inference

1. **Current energy versus future numeric behavior.** At length 32, histories `(a,0,...,0)` and `(b,0,...,0)` with distinct unsigned `a,b` have identical last-31 tails. Their current energies differ, but every nonempty numeric continuation agrees. Thus T1 does not require preserving an already emitted energy for its own sake.
2. **Order versus a multiset or one sum.** At equal length, take padded tails `(1,0,0,...,0)` and `(0,1,0,...,0)` in oldest-to-newest order. Their sums of squares and immediate next energies on a zero input agree; the following zero input produces different energies as the older 1 expires first. A single equal energy, an unordered multiset, or one-step testing is not the T1 equivalence relation.
3. **Numeric equality versus full interface.** Empty history and a one-zero history have the same padded numeric tail. Numeric futures agree; the next logical event indices differ. T1 excludes indices and cannot justify merging full-interface states.
4. **Moving information across ownership boundaries.** A hypothetical exact readback interface can supply the current sequence's needed prior values while the detector consults that service. This breaks the inference from complete-system distinguishability to detector-local history storage. It neither proves a realizable tiny detector nor supplies latency, energy or RAM measurements. The publisher/service must retain or recover the distinguishing information somewhere within a complete boundary.
5. **Finite horizon.** With only one accepted event remaining, the two tails in item 2 cannot be distinguished by the available numeric future. With no remaining events, future numeric equivalence is vacuous. Hence one cannot silently replace unrestricted continuations with a bounded-epoch quantifier and retain the accepted equivalence claim unchanged.
6. **Resource exclusion versus resource absence.** Exclusively upstream ownership can exclude bytes from R5 while those bytes are essential to the computation. It does not set service compute/energy to zero, establish detector RAM fit, or establish full-system resource superiority.

Items 1–6 are boundary examples and conditional countermodels, not implementation proposals or newly accepted proofs.

## 8. Recommendation and disposition handoff

Recommend, **for later delegate consideration only**, retaining logical `n` as the abstract per-sequence association and requiring each later candidate to make its concrete association and information boundary auditable. This preserves the existing PS without mandating external support, internal unbounded arithmetic, an epoch cap, a storage design or a history-service provider. Keep A/B/C open subject to exact observable behavior, R5's existing ownership rule and honest disclosure of consulted information. Do not add PD A4 as a universal AS requirement.

I remains an association-preserving contract possibility, with wider-protocol obligations. II is an explicit optional contract limitation requiring new disposition and reviewed clarification before adoption. III exposes a conditional incompatibility requiring renewed review if demanded; it is not an implementation target selected here. For unresolved shared ownership under C, request an explicit accounting clarification rather than assuming the upstream exemption. Any choice that changes the accepted contract must be versioned and reviewed; any F change must follow F's revision policy.
